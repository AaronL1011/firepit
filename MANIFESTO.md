# Fire, AI and the Firepit.

Fire, its origins interpreted variously across cultures, objectively was the first revolutionary change in humanity's ability to leverage energy, power, and heat into the industrial and utility applications we have today. Held in hands, bounded by a vessel, wielded by its creator.

AI in a similar sense, can be thought of as humanity's next technological revolution - to leverage thought, decision and language into equally industrial and utilitarian form. This is another emergence of a highly powerful, and much like fire, potentially highly dangerous tool - depending on how it is wielded.

AI then, requires its own equivalents of the fire pit and the extinguisher. Simple, versatile, bounded spaces for AI to operate within, granting the people who run it safe, controlled and predictable operation.

This is the high-level goal of Firepit AI. Open, honest and simple standards of tooling for AI operation within broader society, BYO fire.

## Containment today

Containment of AI as it looks today takes one of two forms; **expert-only infrastructure** in the form of sandboxes and containers, often out of reach of newer technologists and the layperson - or **vendor-granted policy**, usage policies and guard rails dictated by those who sell you the model. Even where vendors now ship a sandbox, it wraps the parts they choose, with defaults they set.

These centralised patterns of governance, safety and control do not scale fairly across a broader society, often falling over in accessibility, democracy and trust. They cannot promise to serve everyone fairly or in a way where AI benefits humanity as a whole, as opposed to a select few.

Firepit is neither. It is not infrastructure you must be an expert to operate, nor a policy someone else sets for you. It is a shape any runtime can take: a sandbox whose boundary is declared by the person who runs the AI, readable by anyone, and killable at will.

Containment, utilisation and auditability of AI must be owned by and transparent to the person running it. **The boundary of danger belongs to the one who bears the risk, not the provider of AI.**

## Five properties

A sandbox is only worth trusting if it holds all five of these properties, against the worst agent you could put in it.

- **Bounded.** Every path, address, key, tool and minute the agent may reach is declared up front. Nothing is ambient, nothing is assumed.
- **Legible.** The owner can state the boundary in a single plain sentence. A boundary you cannot understand is not one you own.
- **Killable.** The owner can stop the agent at will: immediately, completely, with nothing left running.
- **Auditable.** Everything that crosses the boundary is recorded, complete and readable by a person, out of the agent's reach.
- **Fail-closed.** When the sandbox breaks, the agent stops.

## Where the analogy ends

Fire's danger is local; it burns what it touches. AI's is not. Give it a network and its effects reach far beyond the machine it runs on: a message sent, a key used, data landing somewhere you will never see.

This is why controlled egress matters most. A sandbox cannot judge what leaves through it. It can only ensure there is one declared way out, that the owner chose where it points, and that every crossing is recorded.

Firepit does not make the AI safe. Nothing honestly can, not while instructions hidden in whatever it reads can turn it against its owner, and not while an agent chasing its goal can find routes nobody intended. What Firepit makes safe is the sandbox, and the sandbox does not ask why the agent is pushing on its boundary.

## Who builds it

The first sandboxes will be built by builders: engineers, toolmakers and the authors of agent frameworks, people who could build one this month. That is where this begins, not who it is for. Legibility is the promise to everyone else: you should not need to understand how a sandbox is built to understand what it holds.

The goal is not a product, nor a policy, nor alignment. Firepit is a shape: open, free to conform to, and indifferent to whichever AI you bring.

A standard nobody has built is not honest. So the first sandbox is being built now, and it must pass muster before we ask anything of anyone else.

Our ancestors, handed fire, built the tools to control it. Firepit asks the same of us for AI.
